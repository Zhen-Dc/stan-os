# Platform Rules

## Shared Rules

- Build trust through process, proof, and useful insight.
- Avoid sounding like the only goal is to get client money.
- Use real proof only when approved. Otherwise anonymize names, logos, and sensitive data.
- Do not promise guaranteed revenue growth. Tie "10x" language to speed, throughput, operating capacity, or decision quality.
- Use practical, direct, proof-driven voice.
- Keep platform drafts editable `.txt` files.
- For carousel platforms, keep slide copy and caption copy separate. `post.txt` can hold the carousel/slide script, while `caption.txt` must hold the paste-ready social caption with the hook at the top.
- Pull every post, slide, and quote-card idea from the video source material. Prefer transcript/script wording when available; otherwise use verified title, description, chapters, and source brief.
- Use lessons, facts, frameworks, or useful takeaways from the video. Do not invent unrelated claims, generic motivational lines, or fake quotes.
- Keep social text close to the approved source or blog. The hook can be sharper, but the body should track the core argument, structure, examples, and terminology from the approved material instead of drifting into generic AI advice.
- Every visual set must include an intro/thumbnail first. For carousels and sliders, this is slide 1. For single-image posts, it is the main thumbnail. Use the established cover pattern: paper/grid background, large topic headline, one colored accent word or number, small badge/pill, creator row, and swipe/slide cue when relevant.
- Open every platform text draft with a catchy, high-friction hook. Use short lines, concrete stakes, curiosity, and a sharp contrast such as "Stop doing X", "I found X", "Here's the fix", or "The expensive mistake is X". Keep hooks truthful to the approved source; do not invent revenue, clients, personal rebuilds, or proof unless the approved source supports it.
- Body copy should use strong creator-style progression: hook, stakes, source-derived proof or walkthrough, concrete steps, and a clean CTA. Use examples such as "Stop writing...", "This video is the best guide on...", "Here's how it works:", and arrow-led step lists as inspiration, but keep every claim tied to the actual source material.

## LinkedIn

Purpose: win B2B trust for AI automation services and educate operators, founders, agencies, and teams.

Post shape:

- Hook: specific manual bottleneck, costly mistake, unexpected insight, or one-line fix.
- Context: what the company/team was doing manually.
- Process: how Stanley mapped departments, found what could be automated, and protected what should stay human.
- Proof: time saved, errors reduced, throughput increased, or cycle time improved.
- Lesson: what the reader can apply.
- CTA: invite a comment, DM, or conversation around automation opportunities.

Visual:

- Animated GIF as the primary visual for process/framework LinkedIn posts, plus a static PNG poster or thumbnail as fallback when useful.
- Use a whiteboard/educational framework feel with numbered steps, arrows, annotations, and small UI sketches.
- Include Stanley's profile picture and a small blue verified badge when available.
- Keep dense text readable on desktop and mobile.

## X

Purpose: sharp AI automation insight and authority.

Post shape:

- One strong quote or contrarian line.
- Optional short thread with 3-6 posts.
- Make the point useful even if the reader never clicks.
- Keep tool names secondary to the operating lesson.

Visual:

- Six 920x458 tweet screenshot-style PNG options by default.
- Use a plain white, content-tight canvas with no extra top or bottom margin, matching the accepted X tweet aspect ratio.
- Place Stanley's brand avatar from `AIS-OS/asset-folder/Brand_Assets/Stan Avatar.png`, the bold name "Stanley", a small blue verified badge after the name, and the @stanleyai handle on one horizontal creator row near the top-left.
- Put large black tweet text below the creator row with generous paragraph spacing.
- Do not include a slide-count pill, border, timestamp, action row, engagement numbers, grid, prompt box, or decorative carousel frame.
- Keep it text-first and uncluttered.
- Use video-derived lessons or facts as the quote text, not random text.

## Instagram

Purpose: educational authority through carousel content.

Post shape:

- Carousel script, 5-8 slides.
- Each slide should feel like a tweet: short, punchy, one idea per card.
- Teach one practical framework or process.
- End with a save/share/comment CTA.
- Also create `drafts/Instagram/text/caption.txt`. The caption must open with a hook, then briefly frame the carousel, then give a save/share/comment CTA.

Visual:

- Tweet-style PNG card or carousel.
- Include Stanley's profile picture and a small blue verified badge when available.
- Place the verified badge beside the creator name with visible spacing; never let it overlap the name.
- Consistent background, clean spacing, readable text.
- Avoid overcrowded slides.

## Facebook

Purpose: course interest and course-sales lane.

Audience: people who may want to learn AI content creation, AI UGC, AI filmmaking, agentic AI, and automation skills.

Post shape:

- Start with a relatable learning or income problem.
- Teach what the YouTube content proves or explains.
- Connect the lesson to skill-building and remote earning potential.
- Keep the tone helpful, not desperate.
- CTA should move toward learning, commenting, joining a waitlist, or asking for the course resource.
- Also create `drafts/Facebook/text/caption.txt`. The caption must open with a strong hook, stay beginner-friendly, and make the lesson feel learnable.

Visual:

- Course-interest graphic or carousel.
- Make the viewer feel, "I can learn this."
- Use beginner-friendly language.
- Use generous top, bottom, and container padding so no headline, row text, or CTA is clipped.
- If a creator row appears, place the blue verified badge beside the name with visible spacing.
- Avoid luxury, hype, or unrealistic income claims.
