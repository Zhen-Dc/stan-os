# Correction Patterns Learned From Stanley's Reviews

Use these as reusable diagnostics, not as public slide copy.

## Strategy And Department Fit

- Lead with the department's work, not with the novelty of the AI tool.
- Name one repeated task, deliverable, or decision the team already owns.
- Show the chain: familiar input -> workflow -> usable output.
- Make the friction concrete: hours, manual steps, meetings, handoffs, waste,
  delays, inconsistency, or risk. Do not invent a number.
- Promise faster or easier completion in specific operational terms. `Save
  time` by itself is too vague.
- A list of prompts or tools only qualifies when every item maps to a real job
  and finished output.
- A model announcement only qualifies when it is immediately converted into a
  department use case.
- Include human review where financial, legal, hiring, publishing, or other
  high-impact decisions require it.
- If a source already fits, improve specificity, proof, and promise. If it does
  not fit, prescribe a department-led recreation instead of approving the
  topic politely.

## Cover

- "Big" is not enough. The title must feel gigantic, heavy, and dominant.
- Force title lines when auto-fit makes the headline weak.
- Keep word spacing readable; words must not collide or overlap.
- Move the title when dead space prevents the subtitle from breathing.
- Subtitle must be readable and usually 3-4 lines when it explains the post.
- Cover is a hook, not a dense teaching slide.
- Preserve Stanley-provided cover wording unless he requests a rewrite.
- Use a real number when the source supports one.

## Slide Copy

- Complete every sentence.
- Keep body copy consistently readable.
- Explain jargon and process labels in plain English.
- Remove internal references such as "the caption gives..."
- Add detail when a slide is vague, but keep one main idea.
- Substantial value should not become a pasted paragraph.
- Each slide should teach when encountered alone.

## Diagrams

- Diagrams must represent the content, not fill space.
- Lists, processes, comparisons, systems, costs, and proof require different
  structures.
- One tool per slide is better when the post promises many tools.
- If an illustration cannot be explained in one sentence, redesign it.
- Flow direction, labels, inputs, outputs, and handoffs must be obvious.
- Text must never leave a box, bottom band, card, or diagram.
- Repeated bottom panels and card grids make the carousel feel static.

## Contrast, Legibility, And Whitespace

- Colored text on white or light cards often needs a darker shade.
- Inspect every title, label, chip, border, and diagram caption on its actual
  background.
- A contact sheet can hide clipping; open flagged slides full size.
- Fix overlaps between title, subtitle, profile row, footer, and diagrams.
- Do not leave large dead bands under titles.
- Use whitespace to improve hierarchy, not hide missing value.
- Fill useful areas with examples, steps, gates, comparisons, inputs, outputs,
  proof, or takeaways without crowding.

## Carousel Structure

- Keep hook -> stakes -> value -> receipts -> CTA as a narrative path.
- Keep visual structures dynamic.
- Expand slide count when each tool, role, or process deserves a slide.
- Teaching slides must not be clones with different words.
- Receipts are visible proof, not another abstract claim.
- CTA slide contains one ask.

## Captions

- Include hook, stakes, useful body, proof, and CTA.
- Keep about 70% of the source caption's effective structure and core content.
- Do not open with a naked keyword command.
- Place the keyword CTA as the final ask.
- Avoid loose summaries and generic "save this" endings.
