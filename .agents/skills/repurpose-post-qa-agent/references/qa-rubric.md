# Post QA Rubric

## Hard Fails

Any item forces `REVISE` or `BLOCKED` regardless of score:

- Essential source caption or image was not analyzed.
- The post does not identify a department or responsible business role.
- The post does not address a specific repeated task, deliverable, or decision.
- The post is mainly an AI tip, model update, tool list, or prompt list without
  a department-level operational use case.
- The post does not make the input, mechanism, and usable output clear.
- The faster/easier promise is vague, unsupported, or disconnected from the
  task.
- Fake ownership, proof, result, or metric.
- Cover title overlaps, clips, or is visually weak.
- Any visible sentence is incomplete, clipped, outside its box, or illegible.
- Low-contrast text blends into its background.
- Diagram is decorative, vague, or unrelated to the slide's meaning.
- Neighboring teaching slides repeat the same structure with swapped text.
- Public copy exposes internal production language.
- Wrong creator identity or fake avatar.
- Keyword CTA is not the final ask.
- Two asks appear on the CTA slide.
- Instagram dimensions are not 1080x1440.
- Final media was not visually inspected.

## Scorecard

### 1. Attention And Hook - 20

- 5: First three seconds create curiosity, tension, or urgency.
- 5: Cover title is gigantic, heavy, concise, and legible.
- 4: Claim is specific and uses a real number when available.
- 3: Subtitle explains the promise in 3-4 readable lines when needed.
- 3: Hook is relevant to the buyer, not generic clickbait.

### 2. Business Problem And Strategy - 15

- 3: Names a department or responsible role and specific buyer.
- 3: Names a recurring task, deliverable, or decision.
- 3: Makes the current time, handoff, cost, consistency, or risk friction clear.
- 3: Shows the concrete input -> mechanism -> usable output chain.
- 3: Makes a credible faster/easier promise tied to a business outcome and
  guardrail.

### 3. Body Tightness And Value - 15

- 4: One main idea per slide.
- 3: Short lines and plain language.
- 3: No throat clearing, filler, vague headers, or repeated explanation.
- 3: Every slide adds enough useful information to teach.
- 2: Sequence is coherent from stakes through value and proof.

### 4. Visual Communication - 15

- 4: Every diagram explains the slide's exact idea.
- 3: Structures vary according to content type.
- 3: Whitespace supports hierarchy and useful information.
- 3: Text is fully contained, legible, and high contrast.
- 2: Illustration labels and flows are immediately understandable.

### 5. Credibility And Proof - 10

- 4: Claims stay within verified evidence.
- 3: Includes receipts, examples, screenshots, or checkable detail.
- 2: Distinguishes mechanism from claimed result.
- 1: Avoids unsupported hype.

### 6. Caption Quality - 10

- 3: Strong natural hook.
- 2: Clear why-now stakes.
- 3: Useful body with structure, proof, and specificity.
- 1: Preserves the reference caption's useful sequence.
- 1: One CTA appears as the final ask.

### 7. Brand And Identity - 5

- 2: `stanley chima` and `@chima-stanley-chukwu` are correct.
- 2: Real Stanley avatar is used cleanly.
- 1: Voice is practical, direct, and proof-driven.

### 8. Package And Technical Completeness - 5

- 2: Required drafts and final media exist.
- 1: Dimensions, counts, numbering, and manifest are valid.
- 1: Contact sheet, QA note, and requested PDF/GIF exist.
- 1: Final edits are reflected in every derivative.

### 9. Audience Feeling And Action - 5

- 2: Reader feels recognized and understood.
- 1: Reader feels relief or capability, not confusion.
- 1: Reader trusts the mechanism enough to continue.
- 1: CTA is a natural next step.

## Interpretation

- `90-100`: PASS, only if no hard fail.
- `80-89`: REVISE; promising but not ready.
- `65-79`: REVISE; weak strategy or execution.
- `<65`: REBUILD; the concept or structure is wrong.

End every report with `LIKELY TO STOP`, `MIXED`, or `LIKELY TO SCROLL`, plus
the likely first thought and feeling. Do not give a polite verdict that
contradicts visible evidence.

## Source Scan Interpretation

When auditing a source post rather than a finished package, return:

- `MEETS`: the department, task, friction, mechanism, output, and credible
  promise are clear. Still prescribe how Stanley can make it more specific,
  useful, and trustworthy.
- `NEEDS REFRAME`: the source is useful but tool-led, generic, incomplete, or
  missing a tangible operational promise. Provide a department-led rewrite
  angle and exact visual direction.
- `BLOCKED`: the caption or attached visual is missing, inaccessible, or
  essential claims cannot be checked.

Always include the target audience lens: likely first thought, first feeling,
whether they stop or scroll, what earns or loses trust, and what would make
them act.
