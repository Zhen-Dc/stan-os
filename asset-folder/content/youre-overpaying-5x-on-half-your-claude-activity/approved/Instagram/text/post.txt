Slide 1: Stop wasting Claude
Half your prompts may cost 5x more when the wrong model or effort level does the job.
COVER: The useful move is model routing: choose the right model, then set how hard it should think.

Slide 2: The real leak
Simple Claude jobs do not need your strongest model.
SETUP_LEAK: Summaries, formatting, classification, quick lookups, and bulk cleanup can quietly burn premium limits when they run on Opus or Fable by default.

Slide 3: Use Haiku for bulk
Haiku is the cheap lane for work where depth does not matter.
INSTALL_FILTER: Route summarising, reformatting, classification, tagging, and repeated cleanup to the lowest-cost lane first.

Slide 4: Make Sonnet default
Sonnet is the everyday workhorse when you need useful output without burning limits fast.
COMMAND_MENU: Use it for drafting, rewrites, turning notes into plans, normal analysis, and most day-to-day Claude sessions.

Slide 5: Save Opus for risk
Opus earns its cost when a wrong answer is expensive.
DECISION_TREE: Move up when the task involves strategy, contracts, multi-step reasoning, high-stakes analysis, or judgment you cannot afford to redo.

Slide 6: Fable is for long builds
Fable should be reserved for agentic work that plans, builds, and tests.
BUILD_LOOP: Use it when the job runs long, has checkpoints, uses tools, creates files, and needs the model to keep improving its own work.

Slide 7: The dial saves limits too
Before upgrading the model, set how hard Claude should think.
TOKEN_SCAN: Low handles quick bulk. Medium is the cost step-down. High is the sweet spot. Extra fits long agentic runs. Max is usually expensive for small gains.

Slide 8: Receipt: 1x to 10x
The model map gives a simple cost ladder: Haiku 1x, Sonnet 3x, Opus 5x, Fable 10x.
RECEIPTS: The checkable rule is not to always choose the cheapest model. It is to keep cheap work cheap and reserve premium models for work where quality changes the outcome.
