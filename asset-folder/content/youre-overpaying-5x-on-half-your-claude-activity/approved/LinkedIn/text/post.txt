
Most people do not run out of Claude because the work is too advanced.

They run out because the wrong model is doing the wrong job.

Charlie Hills made the useful point clearly:

You can overpay 5x on simple Claude prompts and burn limits 2x faster just by picking the wrong model or effort level.

The practical model map:

1. Haiku 4.5
Use it for summaries, formatting, classification, and bulk tasks where depth does not matter.

2. Sonnet 5
Use it as the daily default for drafting, rewrites, notes-to-plan work, and normal thinking.

3. Opus 5
Use it when being wrong is expensive: strategy, contracts, multi-step analysis, and judgment-heavy work.

4. Fable 5
Use it for long agentic builds you can leave running, where the model plans, builds, and tests.

The missed lever is the effort dial.

High is the sweet spot.
Medium is the cost step-down.
Low is for quick bulk work.
Extra is for long agentic runs.
Max is usually the trap.

The cheapest upgrade is not always changing the model.

Sometimes it is moving the dial before you move the model.
