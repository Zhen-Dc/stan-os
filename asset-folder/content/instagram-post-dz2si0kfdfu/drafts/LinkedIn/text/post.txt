Claude is not useful in marketing because you installed 24 random things.

It becomes useful when the stack is built around the work your team repeats every week.

For most marketing teams, the repeated work looks like this:

Research competitors.
Find audience pain points.
Build campaign briefs.
Write posts, emails, scripts, and ad variations.
Summarize performance.
Prepare follow-up.
Check claims before anything goes live.

That is where Claude should sit.

Not as a toy.
Not as a prompt folder.
Not as another tab someone opens when they are stuck.

As a marketing workflow layer.

The setup I would build for an app company, SaaS team, agency, or creator-led business:

1. Research workspace
Claude reads competitors, customer questions, product notes, offers, and past campaign assets.

2. Campaign brief role
Claude turns the research into a buyer, promise, offer angle, channel plan, and CTA.

3. Content production role
Claude creates posts, scripts, emails, landing page sections, and ad variations from the approved brief.

4. Reporting role
Claude turns metrics into a plain-language update: what changed, what worked, and what to test next.

5. Follow-up role
Claude prepares DM replies, lead notes, call prep, and CRM next steps.

6. QA role
Claude checks claims, proof, brand fit, and offer clarity before the human approves.

The goal is not to replace the marketing team.

The goal is to remove the repeated manual work that slows the team down.

If your company is using Claude but every campaign still starts from scratch, you do not have a stack yet.

You have a chat box.

The stack starts when one marketing task becomes a repeatable workflow.
