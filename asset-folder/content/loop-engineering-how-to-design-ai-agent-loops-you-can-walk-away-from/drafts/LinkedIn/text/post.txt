Most AI agents do not fail because the prompt is too short.

They fail because there is no loop.

That is the part most people miss.

A prompt gives AI an instruction.

A loop gives AI an operating cycle:

Understand the goal.
Break it into steps.
Take the next action.
Check the result.
Decide whether to continue, revise, or stop.

That is loop engineering.

It is the shift from asking AI for isolated answers to designing workflows an agent can keep moving through without being micromanaged at every step.

This matters because real work is rarely one response.

Building a feature, reviewing a codebase, researching a topic, repurposing content, cleaning a workflow, or monitoring an operation all require multiple passes.

The agent needs context, allowed actions, feedback checks, boundaries, and a stopping point.

Without that, it is just guessing faster.

A strong AI loop usually has:

1. A clear goal
2. The right context
3. Defined actions
4. Feedback checks
5. Stop conditions
6. Inspectable outputs

The real unlock is not unlimited autonomy.

It is bounded autonomy.

Give the agent a clear lane to work inside, then make it stop when approval, judgment, publishing, deleting, merging, or sending is involved.

Example:

Do not just say, "rewrite this article."

Design the loop:

Fetch the source.
Save the original.
Extract the facts.
Rewrite in the right voice.
Check for false ownership claims.
Create metadata.
Wait for approval.
Repurpose the approved version.

That is a workflow.

The better question is no longer:

"What prompt should I write?"

It is:

"What loop should this AI follow, what should it check, and when should it stop?"

That is how AI moves from helpful responses to repeatable work.
