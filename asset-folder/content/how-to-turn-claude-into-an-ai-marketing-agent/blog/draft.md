# How to Turn Claude Into an AI Marketing Agent

## Hook

Agentic marketing is not just about asking AI to write a caption. The more powerful shift is using an AI agent to research the market, build a content plan, generate creative assets, connect to ad platforms, and help optimize what happens next.

That is the workflow demonstrated in the LinkedIn guide by Deykhan Ten: Claude connected with Higgsfield AI through MCP, used as a single marketing production environment instead of a pile of disconnected tools.

## Introduction

Most marketing teams do not struggle because they lack ideas. They struggle because every stage of execution lives somewhere else.

Research happens in one place. Scripts live in another. Video generation requires another platform. Static creative needs another tool. Ads are launched from a different dashboard. Performance data sits somewhere else again.

The useful lesson from the guide is simple: when Claude can connect to specialized tools through MCP, the chat becomes more than a brainstorming window. It can become the control layer for a full marketing workflow.

The example shows an agentic content factory that moves through research, planning, production, distribution, and optimization. Claude handles the reasoning and orchestration. Higgsfield handles creative generation. Meta Ads becomes part of the launch and performance loop.

## What It Is

This workflow is an AI marketing agent built around Claude, Higgsfield MCP, and a content factory skill.

Instead of manually prompting separate tools one by one, the operator gives Claude a campaign goal and connects it to the systems it needs. Claude can then pull live trend data, organize the campaign, generate a production plan, call the creative generation tool, and prepare assets for distribution.

In the guide, the workflow is organized into four main operating stages:

1. Research: Claude studies live signals from platforms such as TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube to understand what is trending in the niche.
2. Planning: Claude turns the research into a structured content calendar with formats, hooks, scripts, dates, and campaign logic.
3. Production: Higgsfield generates the video and image assets, with approval gates before each batch so the operator stays in control.
4. Distribution: The workflow connects to Meta Ads through MCP so campaign details, scheduling, and launch preparation can happen from the same operating flow.

The guide also adds a fifth business layer: cost analysis. After the assets are produced, Claude is asked to calculate the credit usage and compare the AI-assisted workflow with a traditional production route.

## Why It Matters

The biggest change here is not that AI can generate ads. That part is already familiar.

The real shift is that AI can now connect the steps around the ad: trend research, angle selection, hook generation, creative production, campaign setup, performance reading, and next-step recommendations.

That matters because marketing speed depends on handoffs. A good idea can die slowly when it has to move from strategist to copywriter, from copywriter to designer, from designer to video editor, from editor to media buyer, and from media buyer to analyst.

An agentic workflow compresses those handoffs. The human still decides the strategy and approves the important steps, but the system can carry context from one stage to the next.

For a small team, this means more experiments with fewer production bottlenecks. For a larger team, it means faster campaign assembly and more consistent creative testing.

## Step-by-Step Process

### 1. Connect Claude to the creative engine

The first step is connecting Claude to Higgsfield through MCP. MCP gives Claude access to external tools in a controlled way, so the model can call the creative system instead of merely describing what the user should do elsewhere.

Once connected, Claude becomes the command layer. The user can ask for research, creative concepts, UGC-style ads, product shots, and campaign assets without constantly switching between separate platforms.

### 2. Install or invoke the content factory skill

The guide uses a content factory skill to give Claude a defined operating sequence.

This matters because agentic work needs structure. Without a workflow, the agent may generate ideas but fail to turn them into a complete campaign system. With a skill, the agent knows the stages: research first, content plan second, production third, campaign setup fourth.

That structure keeps the process from becoming a messy collection of prompts.

### 3. Start with live market research

The first real stage is research.

Claude looks at what is currently working across social platforms. The goal is not to guess from memory. The goal is to identify live patterns: trending formats, competitor examples, hook styles, niche-specific angles, and content types that are already converting attention.

In the guide, the output becomes a viral content brief. It includes a trends table, competitor references, working hook patterns, and idea cards.

The important point is that each idea is not just a vague concept. Each card includes production details such as the format, scene direction, hook, duration, and caption logic.

### 4. Turn research into a production calendar

After research, Claude builds the content plan.

This is where the workflow becomes operational. The plan maps out campaign details, date ranges, formats, scripts, hooks, and production volume. In the demo, the agent plans 100 UGC-style videos across five different formats.

That is the kind of document a producer would normally create manually. The difference is that the plan stays connected to the research that produced it.

This reduces one of the biggest problems in content operations: strategy gets written in one document, but production teams later receive disconnected tasks with no memory of why each asset exists.

### 5. Generate assets in controlled batches

The production stage uses Higgsfield to generate the actual creative.

A useful detail from the guide is the use of permission gates. The system does not simply burn through the full generation budget at once. It asks before each batch, which gives the operator room to review, redirect, or stop.

That is how AI production should work in serious marketing environments. Automation is useful, but unchecked automation can create waste. A better setup gives the agent enough freedom to execute while keeping human approval at the points where cost, brand risk, or quality matters.

### 6. Connect the campaign to distribution

Once creative assets are ready, the workflow can move into campaign setup.

The guide connects the process to Meta Ads through MCP, which allows campaign information to be prepared from the same environment. The practical value is context continuity. The same agent that researched the audience and planned the hooks can help prepare the campaign structure.

For teams, this reduces the gap between creative production and media buying.

### 7. Read performance and scale winners

The workflow does not end at launch.

The guide points toward a feedback loop where campaign performance can be read, winning assets can be identified, and the system can generate matching static creatives or new variations from the same campaign context.

This is where agentic marketing becomes especially useful. A normal content workflow often stops once assets are delivered. A stronger workflow watches what performs and feeds that information back into the next production cycle.

## Practical Example

Imagine a skincare brand wants to launch a new product campaign.

The old process might look like this:

- A strategist researches competitors manually.
- A copywriter writes hooks and scripts.
- A designer creates visual directions.
- A creator or video team produces UGC-style ads.
- A media buyer uploads the assets.
- An analyst reviews performance later.
- The team meets again to decide what to produce next.

The agentic version is more connected.

Claude starts by researching what skincare ads are trending on TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube. It identifies repeated hook patterns, popular formats, creator-style angles, and competitor positioning.

Then it builds a content calendar with five ad formats: product demo, testimonial-style UGC, problem-solution hook, routine-based content, and offer-driven short ads.

Next, the connected creative tool generates batches of videos and static assets. The operator reviews each batch before more credits are spent.

After that, the campaign can be prepared for Meta Ads, with naming, structure, dates, and creative mapping already informed by the original research.

Once performance data comes in, Claude can help identify what to scale and what to remake.

The value is not that the human disappears. The value is that the human stops dragging context from one tool to another all day.

## Common Mistakes

### Treating the agent like a magic button

The workflow still needs a clear campaign goal, a defined audience, a product angle, and brand constraints. If those inputs are weak, the agent will produce weak output faster.

### Skipping approval gates

Batch approval is important. It protects budget and brand quality. Letting the system generate everything without review can create unnecessary waste.

### Confusing volume with strategy

Generating 100 videos is only useful if the videos are based on real insight. The research stage matters because it gives production a reason.

### Ignoring brand consistency

AI-generated content can drift quickly. Teams should define tone, visual standards, product claims, compliance rules, and examples of what the brand should never say.

### Forgetting the optimization loop

The strongest part of agentic marketing is not first production. It is the loop after publishing: read performance, identify winners, generate variations, and keep improving.

## Final Takeaway

The future of AI marketing is not one prompt that writes an ad.

It is a connected workflow where an agent can research the market, plan the content, generate assets, prepare distribution, and learn from performance.

The guide shows what that can look like when Claude is connected to Higgsfield and campaign tools through MCP. The important lesson for businesses is not to copy every tool exactly. The lesson is to design a marketing system where each stage passes context to the next.

When research, planning, production, distribution, and optimization live in one operating flow, marketing becomes faster, cheaper to test, and easier to scale.
