Slide 1: Use AI like a team
The time saving comes from assigning roles, not asking one chat to do everything.
COVER: One chat is useful. A role-based workflow is repeatable.

Slide 2: Split the work
A role-based AI team makes the same content job easier to repeat.
PIPELINE: One role researches, one finds the angle, one drafts, one edits, one plans visuals, and one checks the final handoff.

Slide 3: Chat vs team system
One chat creates an answer. A team system creates a repeatable content process.
COMPARISON: Assign each stage a role so the output moves through a clear chain.

Slide 4: AI speed still needs taste
Let AI move the draft forward. Keep human judgment on the final message.
RATIO: AI can run the roles. You still decide if the final post is clear, useful, and worth publishing.

Slide 5: Build the AI team
Start with the job, then assign the roles.
FLOW: Give the researcher the source, the strategist the angle, the writer the draft, the editor the tone, and QA the checklist.

Slide 6: What to keep
The system gets better when the reusable parts are saved.
PROGRESS: Keep role prompts, brand voice, source notes, caption formats, visual rules, and approval criteria.

Slide 7: Why random prompts waste time
The tool is fast, but restarting the process every time is slow.
FIX: Save the roles, inputs, outputs, examples, review rules, and final folder path.

Slide 8: Save this team rule
If the work has stages, do not use one prompt. Use roles.
SAVE THIS: A practical AI team is just a repeatable workflow with named responsibilities.
