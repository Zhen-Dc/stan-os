# Source brief

## Core idea

Claude Skills are reusable command workflows. Instead of typing the same long prompt each time, the instructions, voice, rules, and structure can live inside a Skill.

## Process insight

The stronger Stanley angle is that the command is only the front door. The real asset is the repeatable process behind it:

1. Capture the repeated task.
2. Define the input.
3. Add the role and rules.
4. Add voice and brand context.
5. Define the output.
6. Add QA and approval.
7. Save it as a reusable Skill, SOP, agent, or workflow.

## Proof points

- The source post describes Skills as reusable slash commands.
- The source post emphasizes storing long instructions once instead of retyping them.
- The source post uses practical examples: LinkedIn writing, meeting notes, spreadsheet formatting, and AI text cleanup.

## Audience angles

- LinkedIn: business owners, operators, agencies, and teams who repeat AI instructions and need workflow systems.
- Instagram: educational carousel explaining the difference between prompts, Skills, and operating systems.
- X: concise authority quotes about prompts vs systems.
- Facebook: beginner-friendly course-interest angle for people learning how to turn AI into a practical skill.

## Safety notes

- Do not say Stanley built the source creator's skills.
- Do not say Stanley downloaded or tested the free pack.
- Do not promise income, revenue, or guaranteed business outcomes.
- Keep the claim practical: repeated AI tasks should become reusable workflows.

## What must not be claimed

- "I created the 14 Skills."
- "I tested these exact Skills."
- "These Skills will make you money."
- "Claude Skills work forever without updates."
