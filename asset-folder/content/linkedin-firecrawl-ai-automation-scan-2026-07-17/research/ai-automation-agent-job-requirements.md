## What These Roles Expect The Person To Do

### 1. Build Real AI Automations, Not Just Demos

Companies want people who can turn business problems into working systems.

Common tasks:

- Build automations across business functions.
- Connect tools and systems.
- Automate data capture, routing, documents, approvals, and operational processes.
- Replace manual handoffs and spreadsheet-heavy workflows.
- Build production-grade agents and workflows that real teams use.
- Keep automations running after deployment.

Evidence:

- Rex Moore expects the person to build, operate, monitor, troubleshoot, and improve automations across business functions.
- Box expects the engineer to design, prototype, deliver, monitor, and improve AI-first workflow systems inside production IT standards.
- BELAY expects scalable AI-powered automations, integrations, and workflow solutions across the organization.

### 2. Identify Broken Workflows And Redesign Them

This is one of the strongest repeated requirements.

Common tasks:

- Work with business teams to map current processes.
- Identify friction, waste, repetitive manual work, and bottlenecks.
- Translate business requirements into technical specs.
- Prioritize what to automate first.
- Build roadmaps for AI and automation initiatives.

Evidence:

- Box wants the person embedded with Finance, Legal, People, GTM, Customer Success, and other SMEs to identify processes ready for reinvention.
- Pelago wants the engineer to identify high-friction internal workflows and redesign them as AI-powered systems.
- BELAY wants cross-functional work to identify workflow inefficiencies and develop scalable solutions.

### 3. Build AI Agents And Agentic Workflows

AI agent work appears heavily in the agent roles and increasingly in automation roles.

Common tasks:

- Design AI agents, copilots, subject-matter expert agents, and multi-agent workflows.
- Build conversational, autonomous, and hosted agents.
- Design routing, human-in-the-loop workflows, and agent-to-agent coordination.
- Connect agents to internal APIs, databases, Slack, Salesforce, Notion, Dynamics, Microsoft 365, and other systems.
- Add memory, retrieval, tools, and structured outputs.

Evidence:

- Sarnova expects AI agents using Azure AI Foundry, Microsoft Copilot Studio, Foundry SDK, Agent Framework, Responses API, MCP, RAG, and multi-agent coordination.
- arrivia expects multi-step agent architectures for marketing, brand, analytics, and operations.
- Pelago expects production-grade agents connected to internal APIs, databases, and tools.
- Box explicitly asks for custom agents using MCP servers and CLIs.

### 4. Integrate APIs, Webhooks, SaaS Tools, And Enterprise Platforms

Nearly every serious role requires integration ability.

Common tools and surfaces:

- APIs, webhooks, REST, GraphQL, JSON, OAuth/authentication.
- Python, JavaScript/TypeScript, C#, SQL.
- Salesforce, Slack, Notion, Google Workspace, Asana, Zoom.
- Box, Workday, NetSuite, Atlassian, BigQuery.
- Dynamics 365, Dataverse, Azure Functions, Power Platform.
- ERP, CRM, enterprise platforms, iPaaS tools, and custom services.

Evidence:

- Rex Moore asks for APIs, webhooks, connectors, structured JSON, ERP/CRM integration, Python, SQL, and Git.
- Sarnova asks for APIs, MCP, Power Platform, Dataverse, Dynamics 365, Azure Functions, custom services, SQL Server, SharePoint, and CI/CD.
- Box asks for integrations across Box, Workday, Salesforce, NetSuite, Atlassian, BigQuery, APIs, iPaaS, and custom services.
- arrivia asks for deep API and webhook expertise.

### 5. Use LLMs, RAG, Prompting, And Evaluation

The requirements go beyond "use ChatGPT."

Common tasks:

- Use LLM APIs inside workflows.
- Write effective prompts and structured outputs.
- Build RAG and knowledge-grounding systems.
- Use vector search, embeddings, and citation-based responses.
- Evaluate model performance, quality, cost, safety, and reliability.
- Add observability, guardrails, and evaluation frameworks.

Evidence:

- Sarnova asks for Azure AI Search, vector search, embeddings, citation-based responses, prompt engineering, context management, memory, model evaluation, guardrails, and governance.
- Pelago asks for LLM-powered workflows, tool use, retrieval, structured outputs, orchestration, observability, guardrails, and evaluation.
- Box asks for LLM APIs, prompt engineering, RAG, agent frameworks, and MCP.
- Rex Moore asks for AI/LLM workflows, prompts, and AI provider APIs in production workflows.

### 6. Monitor, Troubleshoot, And Own Production Systems

Companies are not only hiring builders. They want operators.

Common tasks:

- Monitor deployed automations.
- Troubleshoot workflow failures.
- Maintain runbooks and operational playbooks.
- Track incidents, reliability, usage, and performance.
- Improve systems after deployment.
- Handle retries, errors, and edge cases.

Evidence:

- Rex Moore says the person should own day-to-day health, monitor, troubleshoot, fix, and improve automations.
- Sarnova expects monitoring, troubleshooting, evaluation, reliability, safety, and performance improvement.
- Box expects monitoring, runbooks, incident response, and continuous improvement.
- BELAY expects ongoing administration, optimization, support, documentation, and workflow standards.

### 7. Measure Business Impact

The roles repeatedly connect automation work to business outcomes.

Common metrics:

- Time saved.
- Error reduction.
- Operational efficiency.
- Workflow reliability.
- Cost and performance.
- Adoption.
- KPIs.
- Business value.
- Capacity unlocked.

Evidence:

- BELAY asks for KPIs to measure effectiveness and business impact.
- Sarnova wants AI-driven solutions that provide measurable business value.
- Box wants systems that materially change how teams operate and transform business workflows.
- Rex Moore wants practical automations that save time and reduce errors.

### 8. Document, Train, And Enable Teams

This is a major hidden requirement. Companies need the automation person to make the system usable by non-technical teams.

Common tasks:

- Write documentation, SOPs, playbooks, workflow guides, and technical specs.
- Teach non-technical colleagues how to use new tools.
- Build training materials and workshops.
- Create prompt libraries and best-practice guides.
- Support change management and adoption.

Evidence:

- BELAY expects training programs, workshops, learning resources, centralized documentation, playbooks, workflow guides, prompts, and best practices.
- Sarnova expects technical documentation, testing assets, operational playbooks, and implementation standards.
- Rex Moore expects clear communication, tool training, and documentation people actually follow.
- Box expects specs, docs, updates, and trust-building with non-technical partners.

## The Jobs Companies Are Actually Hiring This Person To Do

If you compress the listings into plain English, the person is expected to:

1. Find repeated manual work inside the company.
2. Map the workflow with the team doing the work.
3. Decide where AI should and should not be used.
4. Build the automation with APIs, workflow tools, code, and AI agents.
5. Connect the automation to business systems.
6. Add prompts, RAG, tools, memory, routing, and human review where needed.
7. Test the automation with real cases.
8. Add logging, monitoring, guardrails, and error handling.
9. Train staff on how to use it.
10. Document the workflow so it can be maintained.
11. Track KPIs like time saved, error reduction, and adoption.
12. Keep improving the automation after launch.

The market is not just asking for "AI prompt experts." It is asking for practical AI automation builders who can:

- understand business operations,
- build working systems,
- integrate tools,
- document the workflow,
- train teams,
- and prove business impact.
