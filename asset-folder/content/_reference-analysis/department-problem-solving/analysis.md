# Department Problem-Solving Content Reference Analysis

## Governing Standard

The content lane is department-specific operational improvement. Each post must
help a company role complete a repeated task, deliverable, or decision faster,
easier, more consistently, or with clearer next steps. AI is the mechanism, not
the subject.

Required chain:

```text
Department -> repeated task -> friction -> familiar input -> mechanism ->
usable output -> faster/easier outcome -> proof or guardrail
```

## Reference 1: Five ChatGPT Agents For Creative Strategy

- Verdict: `MEETS`.
- Department: creative strategy/performance creative.
- Task: turn product and customer evidence into a creative test plan.
- Input: one product URL plus reviews.
- Mechanism: five specialist agents hand work forward like a relay.
- Output: language bank, 15 ranked angles, 40 hooks, and shoot-ready briefs.
- Why it works: the caption explains the handoffs; the image makes all five
  roles, their outputs, and the final testing outcome visible at once.
- Improvement for Stanley: lead with the delay removed from creative research
  and briefing, then show where a strategist approves the winning angles.
- Audience reaction: a creative lead is likely to feel recognized and relieved.
  The concrete outputs make this likely to stop the scroll.

## Reference 2: Ten Skills For Performance Marketers

- Verdict: `MEETS`.
- Department: paid media/performance marketing.
- Task: complete recurring Meta and Google Ads jobs each week.
- Input: live ad data, product page, and customer reviews.
- Mechanism: one reusable slash-command skill per task.
- Output: audits, scripts, concepts, grades, and scale/pause/test/rebuild calls.
- Why it works: the caption promises minutes; the terminal visual groups the
  jobs by platform and names the finished result of every command.
- Improvement for Stanley: organize the carousel around the weekly paid-media
  operating rhythm and add a final human approval gate before account changes.
- Audience reaction: practical and immediately useful; likely to earn saves and
  comments from performance marketers.

## Reference 3: Automated Ad Account Audit Prompts

- Verdict: `MEETS`, with a proof warning.
- Department: paid media/account management.
- Task: audit Meta and Google Ads exports and decide what to do next.
- Input: account exports.
- Mechanism: ten focused audits with ranked findings.
- Output: scale, pause, test, or rebuild verdicts.
- Why it works: it solves the decision after the audit, not just the analysis.
  The image demonstrates a ranked action table and monthly-waste calculation.
- Guardrail: treat the figures shown in the visual as an example unless a real
  account export verifies them. Do not reuse the dollar amount as Stanley's
  proof.
- Improvement for Stanley: promise a prioritized review queue, with account
  changes made only after a media buyer approves the recommendations.
- Audience reaction: strong urgency because wasted spend is concrete; likely to
  stop, but trust drops if the example numbers are presented as a real result.

## Reference 4: Ten Claude Skills For Marketing

- Verdict: `MEETS`, but it is the broadest and weakest-positioned reference.
- Department: marketing across campaign, landing page, testing, email, content,
  and creative tasks.
- Task: convert repeated marketing inputs into finished assets and audits.
- Input: campaign data, URLs, test results, ICP, offers, content, and briefs.
- Mechanism: reusable skills with the workflow baked in.
- Output: ranked fixes, nurture sequences, repurposed content, and ad variants.
- Why it works: both caption and image use a simple input -> output promise for
  each job.
- Improvement for Stanley: split this into department or workflow-specific
  posts. One post for lifecycle marketing, one for campaign analysis, and one
  for content operations would feel more credible than `marketing` as a whole.
- Audience reaction: useful enough to save, but a senior buyer may see it as a
  generic list until one workflow is shown in depth.

## Reference 5: Sonnet Skills For Performance Marketers

- Verdict: `MEETS`, with a model-first weakness.
- Department: paid media/performance marketing.
- Task: find tracking gaps, campaign waste, fatigue, and growth opportunities.
- Input: live Meta and Google Ads data.
- Mechanism: ten audit skills.
- Output: ranked fixes, grades, and scale/hold/kill decisions.
- Why it works: the visual maps each command to a precise account problem.
- Improvement for Stanley: make the account bottleneck the hook and Sonnet the

  quickly and matters less than the operational outcome.
- Audience reaction: performance marketers will recognize the problems and
  stop; business leaders may scroll unless wasted spend, slow reporting, or
  decision delay is brought forward.

## Reference 6: Ten Skills For Creative Strategists

- Verdict: `MEETS`.
- Department: creative strategy and ad production.
- Task: analyze a winning ad and turn it into new testable creative work.
- Input: existing ads, comments, and performance numbers.
- Mechanism: five research skills followed by five production skills.
- Output: scores, angle analysis, language bank, ranked hooks, scripts, briefs,
  variations, and sound/caption direction.
- Why it works: the caption explains the reuse of existing evidence; the image
  clearly separates research from production.
- Guardrail: `one winning ad into 30` is a quantity promise, not proof that all
  30 will perform. Strategy and review still determine test quality.
- Improvement for Stanley: visualize the research-to-production handoff and
  promise a ready-to-test creative queue, not automatic winning ads.
- Audience reaction: strong recognition and capability; likely to stop and save
  because every item maps to familiar work.

## Pattern To Reuse

The best references do six things:

1. Call out a role or department.
2. Name work that role already repeats.
3. Use an input the team already possesses.
4. Break the workflow into specialist jobs or reusable commands.
5. Promise a finished work product or decision, not a chat response.
6. State a credible speed or simplicity benefit and show proof or a guardrail.

When Stanley says `scan this post`, the QA agent must judge this pattern first,
then critique the caption and visual, prescribe a rewrite if needed, and report
the target audience's likely thought, feeling, trust, and stop-or-scroll action.
